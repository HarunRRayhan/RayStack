# Humanize checklist

Run the draft through these in order. Read each paragraph out loud in your head. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

## 1. Cut the AI tells

- **Em dashes.** Use commas, periods or parentheses. Keep at most one em dash per 500 words, and only if it earns its place.
- **Triads.** "Fast, safe, and flexible." Pick the one that is true and show it, or cut all three.
- **Hedges and filler.** "It's worth noting that", "in essence", "at its core", "simply put", "ultimately". Say the thing.
- **Corporate verbs.** leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, unlock, delve, robust, seamless, cutting-edge. Use plain verbs: use, help, let, run.
- **"Not just X, but Y."** Keep the stronger half.
- **Closing summaries.** No "In summary" or "To conclude". If it ended, it ended.

## 2. Use contractions

Write `it's`, `don't`, `you'll`, `I've`. Spelled-out forms read like a contract.

## 3. Short, concrete sentences

One idea per sentence. A sentence with two commas and an "and" becomes two sentences. A paragraph over 4 lines on screen is probably two.

## 4. Numbers, errors, paths

Replace adjectives with the thing itself.

- Bad: "A powerful tool that handles your deploys."
- Good: "Run `make deploy`. It takes about 40 seconds and rolls back on a failed health check."

Quote the exact error text, the exact flag, the real number.

## 5. Cut filler

Delete intros that restate the title, transitions that add nothing ("Moving on to"), and sentences that only announce the next one. Aim for 20 to 30 percent shorter, without losing facts.

## 6. No royal "we"

Say "I" when one person did it and "you" when talking to the reader. Use "we" only when it's a real group.

## 7. Don't oversell

No "blazing fast", "super simple", "just works". Honest caveats beat cheerleading: "On a 2 GB host the build can run out of memory."

## 8. Headings and lists

- Headings should let a reader skip the section. "Overview" and "Introduction" say nothing; "Install on Linux" does.
- Use a list for parallel items. If the items flow into each other, write prose. Fifteen bullets is a wall.

## 9. Humor

One dry aside per document at most. If you wonder whether it lands, cut it.

## Don't touch

Code blocks, commands, file paths, config keys, tables that are already terse, license text, quotes, links, and anything the user asked to keep.

## Before you finish

- [ ] At most one em dash per 500 words
- [ ] No adjective triads
- [ ] No corporate verbs, no "seamless" or "robust"
- [ ] Contractions present
- [ ] No closing summary
- [ ] Every heading says something specific
- [ ] Commands, paths and numbers unchanged
